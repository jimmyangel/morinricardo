*From the Drawer to the Gift Package: The Story of TicTalk*

Every maker has a project like this. You order the parts, get excited when they arrive, and then put them straight into a drawer where they sit for years.

TicTalk started back in 2022 on our way home from California. We stopped by a friend's house, and he showed us a cool word clock he’d built in Swiss German. He used an LED matrix behind a physical stencil mask to light up the words. It looked fantastic and inspired me on the spot. His single-language physical mask setup was super clean, but since I wanted my clock to be multilingual, a static front stencil wouldn't work. Trying to cram multiple languages onto one physical mask was just not feasible. 

That got me thinking: why not skip the mask and write the words directly onto a high-resolution 64x64 LED matrix using code? Standard 64x64 matrices have plenty of pixels for clean text, and instead of a Raspberry Pi like my friend used, I could run the whole thing on an ESP32 micro-controller, which should have plenty of compute power to do the job. And dot matrix fonts are kind of retro and cool looking.

When I got home, I couldn't wait to test the core logic. I had a little M5StickC Plus ESP32 module sitting around, so I hacked together a base word clock prototype in English and Spanish, where you just clicked its physical button to toggle between languages. (That early code still lives over on my [GitHub repository](https://github.com/jimmyangel/WordClock).)

Next, I ordered the real hardware from [Electrodragon](https://www.electrodragon.com/): a [64x64 HUB75 matrix](https://www.electrodragon.com/product/rgb-full-color-led-matrix-panel/), an [ESP32 board](https://www.electrodragon.com/product/esp32-devkitc/), and an [interface shield](https://www.electrodragon.com/product/rgb-matrix-panel-drive-interface-board-for-esp32-dma/) that plugged straight onto the display so I wouldn't have to deal with a mess of wires. Cheap and easy. The parts arrived. I opened the box, looked at everything... and decided it looked like a pretty big job.

So, I put them in a drawer. And there they sat. For three full years!

<p align="center">
	<img src="/images/uploads/wordclock-drawer.jpg"/>
</p>

Fast forward to earlier this year, I decided it was time to dust off those parts and finish it. I'd been using AI for coding lately, so I brought in Gemini as a development buddy to help work through the firmware. Reusing my old M5Stick logic as a starting point, I had the matrix up and running in just a few weeks.

Then I realized something: in our family, we have a tradition of making our own Christmas gifts. I thought these word clocks would make great presents, so I ordered two additional sets of parts from Electrodragon. I expanded the code to support French and German, added a capacitive touch sensor on a brass standoff to cycle through languages, built a simple Wi-Fi setup portal, and designed a [custom 3D-printed enclosure](https://www.tinkercad.com/things/3eDYtOKifTp-worldclock-model-2).

I named it TicTalk, and after almost four years sitting in a drawer, they are finally neatly packaged and ready as Christmas gifts.

## TicTalk Quick Start: Zero Wiring Required

Now that TicTalk is built and ready to gift, here is a quick rundown of how to actually interact with it day-to-day.

### Powering On & Controls
* **Power Switch:** Use the push-button switch on the back to turn the clock on or off. The built-in fuse holder keeps everything safe.
* **Changing Languages:** A quick tap on the brass standoff on the back of the clock cycles through the four available languages: Español, English, Français, Deutsch

Your selection is saved automatically in non-volatile storage, so it remembers your preferred language even if you unplug it.

### 2. Entering Setup Mode (Wi-Fi & Timezone)

**Automatic Entry:**  
If you are powering on a fresh clock for the first time or if TicTalk cannot find its saved Wi-Fi network, it will enter Setup Mode automatically.

**Manual Entry:**  
If you need to change networks later or update your timezone settings, perform a **long hold** (over 3 seconds) on the brass touch standoff on the back of the clock to manually force Setup Mode.

**Setup Steps:**
- Once in Setup Mode, the clock launches a local Wi-Fi network named something like `TicTalk-XXXX`.
- Connect to `TicTalk-XXXX` using your phone, tablet, or laptop.
- A setup page will pop up automatically. Select your home Wi-Fi network, enter your password, and choose your regional timezone (with automatic DST handling, or custom numeric offsets, just in case politicians finally decide to abolish DST).
- Save your settings. TicTalk will automatically reboot, connect to your network, and sync the time via NTP.

### Ambient Color Transitions
Hey, did you notice how the text colors shift throughout the day? Nope, you are not imagining it! TicTalk features time-of-day normalized color blending. It transitions smoothly from bright white in the morning (6 AM) through warm oranges and soft light blues, settling into a deep night blue by midnight.
